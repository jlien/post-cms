Once rendering is a build step instead of a request handler, your server's
job is to be *boring*. The CDN does the actual work.

## Header policy

| Content type | Cache-Control |
|---|---|
| HTML pages | `public, max-age=300, s-maxage=3600` |
| CSS / assets | `public, max-age=31536000, immutable` (content-hashed) |
| RSS / sitemap | `public, max-age=900` |

## The purge loop

The one real risk in a static-first stack is **stale pages after a
frequent publishes**. The fix is a 20-line webhook:

1. Agent merges the publish PR.
2. CI builds `dist/` and rsyncs to the origin.
3. A `purge.sh` call clears the CDN's HTML cache.

Miss that step and reviewers see yesterday's site. It has to exist from
day one — but it's a script, not a platform feature.

## Result

99%+ of requests never reach the origin. The $5 VPS could be a Raspberry
Pi in a closet and nobody would notice.
